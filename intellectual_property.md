# Intellectual Property (IP) Strategy

This document outlines the strategy for protecting the intellectual property of the BioSignal project while maintaining open-source collaboration.

## 1. Dual-Licensing Strategy
- **Software (Open-Source):** The Python simulations, TinyML training code, and documentation are released under the **MIT License**. This encourages academic collaboration and allows other researchers to build upon our work.
- **Hardware & Algorithm (Proprietary/Patentable):** The specific hardware integration (PCB layout and form factor) and the optimized low-power CS-TinyML algorithm are considered proprietary. A patent application may be filed to protect the commercial rights.

## 2. Novelty for Patent Filing
The innovation that could be patented includes:
- A method for dynamic switching between "compressed sensing mode" and "full data mode" based on the patient's activity level.
- The specific integration of a Random Forest classifier on a low-power MCU for real-time arrhythmia detection without cloud dependency.

## 3. Open-Source Ethics
- We believe in the power of open science. All non-proprietary research, data analysis, and academic publications will remain freely accessible.
- We will not patent life-saving algorithms in a way that restricts access for developing countries. The goal is to make the technology affordable for everyone.

## 4. Next Steps
- Consult with a patent attorney after the successful completion of the clinical trials.
- Submit a provisional patent application (PPA) before public disclosure of the final hardware design.
